OutRunners 1.4.2: TYRE SLIP, the tyres sliding in a bend felt on a racing wheel or a game controller, and the car
as the computers' games' icon. As before, nothing of the game comes in these packages: keep your own
`orunners.zip` (MAME's ROM set, World) beside the game, and it takes its assets out of it the first time it starts.
The packages, each platform and device in its own: `outrunners-v1.4.2-windows-x64.zip`,
`outrunners-v1.4.2-linux-x64.tar.gz`, `outrunners-v1.4.2-macos-universal.zip`,
`outrunners-v1.4.2-raspberrypi-arm64.tar.gz`, `outrunners-v1.4.2-switch.zip`, `outrunners-v1.4.2-android.zip`,
`outrunners-v1.4.2-ios.zip`, `outrunners-v1.4.2-anbernic.zip`, `outrunners-v1.4.2-r36s.zip`.

**New**

- **OPTIONS > STEERING > TYRE SLIP** (#48): while your tyres slide past their grip in a bend (the screech and the
  smoke), a racing wheel with force feedback shakes lightly and its centring spring eases, as a wheel goes light
  when the tyres let go (as Cannonball-DX does for OutRun), a game controller rumbles, and on Android's touch
  controls the phone itself vibrates (PHONE VIBRATION). 20% by default, up to 100%; 0% turns it off. A crash's and
  the road's edge's shake (VIBRATION) still goes first. The cabinet had nothing of the kind: the arcade game is as
  it was.

**Changed**

- **The computers' games' icon** (Windows, macOS, Linux) is the car alone, without the sunset behind it; the
  devices' (Switch, Android, iOS) keep the sunset, as their systems want an icon opaque to its edges.
- **STEERING > WHEEL ROTATION** is a computer's alone now, beside CENTRING and SET UP WHEEL: it is a racing
  wheel's turn, and on Android, iOS, the Switch and the handhelds it only made the stick turn less when raised.
  There the stick always turns the game's wheel all the way.

Your saves and settings stay as they are; TYRE SLIP starts at 20%. The assets are 1.4.0's: on computers the game
keeps them, and the devices' games made with 1.4.0's and 1.4.1's tools are still taken.

TYRE SLIP was tried on a phone's touch controls. Not checked: a real racing wheel or game controller (checked on
Windows with a stand-in for SDL's force feedback). How it feels on yours is welcome on #48.

`README.txt` in each package says what its tools need. What a tool makes is yours alone: never share it.
